# Niche big ideas

Use Postiv's inspiration library to identify the big ideas behind winning posts in the user's niche, then develop an original contribution in their voice. Follow the parent skill's workspace/profile selection and shared rules.

Example: “Find what's working in my niche and help me make my own take on a winning big idea.”

## Research the niche

For other creators' work, use `search_inspiration` with the user's niche and relevant filters. Infer the niche from reliable profile/workspace context when clear; otherwise ask. Read returned copy and inspect previews when format matters. Use performance evidence actually returned; do not call something a winner solely because it ranked in semantic search. If evidence is limited, describe it as an interesting candidate. This is a curated library search, not an exhaustive live scan of LinkedIn.

## Separate the idea from the execution

For each shortlisted post, identify the audience problem, central claim or tension, and why the framing may have resonated. Treat performance explanations as hypotheses, not proven causes. Distinguish:

- Big idea: the useful insight or question.
- Execution: the original author's wording, hook, anecdote, example, and design.
- Fresh contribution: the user's own evidence, viewpoint, updated lesson, or different audience application.

Show a few candidates with source links and relevant metrics, followed by a proposed fresh angle for each. If the user authorized you to choose and draft, make the choice and explain it briefly rather than adding an unnecessary selection step.

## Ground the idea in the user's expertise

Transfer the underlying idea into the user's real context. Ask a targeted question or use `search_knowledge` for supporting experience. For example: “This post argues that more onboarding steps reduce activation. Where have you seen that happen, and what did you do differently?”

Do not imitate distinctive copy, paraphrase sentence by sentence, or claim the creator's results as the user's. Keep attribution where a specific borrowed claim, quotation, or framework needs it. A new hook alone is not a new contribution: change the evidence, viewpoint, application, or explanation in a meaningful way.

Use the parent skill's production guidance for the selected post or carousel. Deliver the complete new content, the source inspiration, and a short explanation of what is fresh. If the source idea cannot be grounded in the user's experience, offer a clearly framed commentary angle or ask for evidence instead of fabricating a success story.
