# Subject interview

Interview the user about a subject and turn their expertise into content answering audience questions. Follow the parent skill's workspace/profile selection and shared rules.

Example: “Interview me about why onboarding projects fail, then help me write about it.”

## Conduct the interview

For an interview, establish the subject and intended audience from the user's request and profile context. Start with one concrete question, such as “What do customers keep asking you about this?” Ask one or two follow-ups at a time, adapting to the answer. Look for a real situation, the decision made, what happened, and what the user learned. Explore a surprising opinion or common misconception when one emerges. Do not ask a fixed questionnaire or supply invented answers. Stop once there is enough substance for the requested content, or when the user asks you to draft.

Use relevant profile voice, workspace knowledge, or existing pillars to make questions specific, but let the user's answers change the direction. Reflect back the strongest insight and check any ambiguity before treating it as the user's position. If they only asked to be interviewed, finish with an insight summary and proposed angles; drafting or saving is a continuation, not automatic.

## Find the questions worth answering

Extract audience questions, recurring objections, practical mistakes, and moments where the user explained something unusually well. For each promising angle, identify:

- The audience's question, whether explicitly asked or inferred.
- The user's answer or point of view.
- The concrete example or source passage that supports it.
- What the reader could do differently after reading the post.

If the strongest answer is incomplete, ask a targeted follow-up rather than filling in a result, quote, or statistic. For example: “You said the handoff changed everything. What did you change, specifically?”

## Develop the content

If the user asked to choose from ideas, present a short shortlist and let them select. If they already asked you to select and draft, choose the strongest supported angle and proceed. Avoid turning the interview into a chronological Q&A recap; make each post answer one useful question.

Use the parent's content-production guidance for voice, drafting, and optional carousels. Keep evidence notes outside the public copy so it reads naturally while remaining reviewable. Deliver the full draft, the interview insight it develops, and the Postiv link if saved. Retain unused ideas in Postiv only when requested.
