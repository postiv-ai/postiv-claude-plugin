# Repurpose old winners

Use the user's analytics to find old high-performing posts and develop fresh content from them. Follow the parent skill's workspace/profile selection and shared rules.

Example: “Find three old bangers of mine we could revisit.”

## Find the user's winners

For the user's own posts, call `list_analytics_accounts` and match the selected profile to its `accountId`. Use `get_top_posts` with the requested time window and a sort fitting the goal: reach, engagement, or the available outlier measures. For an unspecified search through old winners, state the chosen window; an all-time search is useful when no narrower range was requested. Inspect actual copy and returned metrics. Use `get_post_analytics`, `get_analytics_summary`, or `get_performance_overview` only when needed to establish context or compare periods. Keep the selected account and periods consistent. Metrics are cached, and a top-post sample is not the whole account.

## Separate the idea from the execution

For each shortlisted post, identify the audience problem, central claim or tension, and why the framing may have resonated. Treat performance explanations as hypotheses, not proven causes. Distinguish:

- Big idea: the useful insight or question.
- Execution: the original author's wording, hook, anecdote, example, and design.
- Fresh contribution: the user's own evidence, viewpoint, updated lesson, or different audience application.

Show a few candidates with source links and relevant metrics, followed by a proposed fresh angle for each. If the user authorized you to choose and draft, make the choice and explain it briefly rather than adding an unnecessary selection step.

## Add a fresh contribution

Revisit the selected post with something substantive: what changed since publication, a deeper example, a counterargument, a narrower audience question, or a different format that adds value. Ask the user what they have learned since writing it when the update depends on their experience. Verify dated claims before reusing them. Check relevant existing drafts/plans when duplication is a concern.

Do not promise that reusing an idea will reproduce its original reach. A large audience, timing, or an unusual event may explain performance; distinguish evidence from speculation. Use the user's metric goal when selecting candidates rather than treating raw likes as the only measure.

Use the parent skill's production guidance for the selected post or carousel. Deliver the complete new content, the source inspiration, and a short explanation of what is fresh. If the source idea cannot be grounded in the user's experience, offer a clearly framed commentary angle or ask for evidence instead of fabricating a success story.
